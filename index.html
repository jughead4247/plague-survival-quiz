About the Plague Survival Test

How would you survive if a dangerous infectious disease suddenly began spreading through your city? The Plague Survival Test places you in a fictional outbreak where normal life begins breaking down and medicine, food, water, shelter, information, and trust become increasingly difficult to manage.

The test contains 40 difficult decisions with four choices each. Your decisions affect two results: a Survival Score, which reflects how effectively you manage danger and resources, and a Morality Score based on choices involving compassion, fairness, responsibility, loyalty, and sacrifice.

There is no single perfect approach. Protecting one person may increase risks for others, while helping strangers may consume resources your own group needs. The test is designed around these conflicts rather than simply rewarding the most aggressive survival strategy.

Infection Control and Essential Resources

During an infectious disease outbreak, reducing unnecessary exposure can be extremely important. Depending on the disease, useful precautions may include avoiding close contact with sick people, improving ventilation, washing hands, cleaning contaminated areas, separating symptomatic people, and following reliable public-health guidance.

Isolation separates people who are sick from those who are not, while quarantine restricts the movement or contact of people who may have been exposed. Both can help reduce transmission when used appropriately.

Long outbreaks can also disrupt food, water, medicine, transport, and other supplies. Water is needed for drinking, cooking, hygiene, sanitation, and cleaning, while food planning must consider how long supplies will last and how they can be replaced.

Medicine can become especially difficult when supplies are limited or healthcare facilities are overwhelmed. Real medical decisions depend on the disease, patient condition, available treatment, and professional guidance. The quiz simplifies these situations for entertainment.

Evacuation, Shelter and Community Survival

Leaving an infected city may appear to be the safest option, but evacuation can create new problems. Crowded roads, fuel shortages, transportation failures, and contact with large numbers of people can increase risk.

A remote shelter may reduce contact with others, but isolation can mean limited medical care, electricity, communication, tools, and food. Urban areas may provide better medical resources while also creating greater exposure risks.

Long-term shelter therefore requires more than four walls. Clean water, sanitation, food, waste management, medical knowledge, communication, and people with useful skills can all become important.

Communities can also be more resilient than isolated individuals. Doctors, engineers, farmers, mechanics, electricians, and other skilled people can help maintain essential systems when normal services fail.

Quiz Navigation

The test begins with the first outbreak decision and moves through 40 scenarios. Use the BACK button to review an earlier decision and NEXT to continue. When all decisions are complete, submit the test to receive your results.

Your result includes a Survival Score, Morality Score, survival category, and estimated survival period. The survival categories range from Extremely Vulnerable to Master Survivor, while the morality result ranges from Ruthless Pragmatist to Humanitarian.

After seeing your result, you can select TRY AGAIN to make different choices. You can also use SHARE MY RESULT or CHALLENGE YOUR FRIENDS to compare your outbreak decisions with other players.

If you want another survival scenario, the suggested quizzes below the result screen include zombie, nuclear-apocalypse, and general apocalypse-preparedness challenges.

Plague Survival Test FAQ

How many decisions are in the test?

There are 40 decisions, and every decision provides four possible choices.

How is the Survival Score calculated?

Your choices contribute to a Survival Score based on how effectively you handle infection risk, resources, shelter, travel, medical situations, and long-term survival. The final result is displayed as a percentage.

What does the Morality Score measure?

It reflects patterns in decisions involving compassion, fairness, responsibility, loyalty, sacrifice, and helping others. It does not determine whether you are a good or bad person.

What are the survival result levels?

Your result falls into one of five categories: Extremely Vulnerable, Short-Term Survivor, Survivor, Outbreak Survivor, or Master Survivor. An estimated survival period is also provided.

Is this test medically accurate?

No. It is a fictional entertainment experience, not medical advice or a professional emergency-planning tool. Real infectious diseases vary greatly, so actual outbreaks require guidance from qualified medical and public-health authorities.

Can I take the test again?

Yes. Use TRY AGAIN after receiving your result and make different choices to see how your Survival and Morality results change.
