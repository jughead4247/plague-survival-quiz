About the Plague Survival Test

How would you survive if a dangerous infectious disease suddenly began spreading through your city? The Plague Survival Test puts you into a fictional outbreak where normal life begins breaking down and information, medicine, food, water, shelter, and trust become increasingly difficult to manage.

This survival test contains 40 difficult decisions. Some situations focus on avoiding infection and managing scarce resources, while others involve evacuation, quarantine, medical treatment, strangers, family members, and the difficult choices that can appear when an outbreak lasts for weeks or months.

Your choices produce two different results. The Survival Score measures how effectively you manage danger and resources, while the Morality Score reflects decisions involving compassion, fairness, responsibility, loyalty, sacrifice, and other human values.

There is no single perfect personality for every situation. A decision that protects one person may increase risk for another, while helping someone may consume resources your own group desperately needs. The test is designed around those conflicts rather than simply rewarding aggressive survival behavior.

Infection Control During a Disease Outbreak

During a real infectious disease outbreak, reducing exposure is often more important than taking dramatic actions. Disease transmission depends on the pathogen and its route of spread, but crowded environments, close contact, contaminated surfaces, unsafe water, poor sanitation, and inadequate isolation can all create additional risks for certain diseases.

Basic infection-control measures can therefore become extremely important. Depending on the disease, these may include staying away from sick people, improving ventilation, using appropriate protective equipment, washing hands, cleaning contaminated areas, separating symptomatic people, and following public-health guidance.

Quarantine and isolation are also different concepts. Isolation separates people who are sick from those who are not, while quarantine restricts movement or contact for people who may have been exposed. During a major outbreak, these measures can reduce transmission, but they work best when they are based on reliable information and implemented fairly.

The safest decision is not always the most dramatic one. Avoiding unnecessary exposure, monitoring symptoms, and obtaining reliable information can prevent a manageable situation from becoming a much more dangerous one.

Food, Water, Medicine and Essential Resources

A prolonged outbreak can create problems even when the disease itself is not directly affecting everyone. Transport disruptions, worker shortages, panic buying, closed businesses, damaged supply chains, and restrictions on movement can make ordinary necessities harder to obtain.

Water is especially important because drinking is only one part of household water demand. Cooking, cleaning, hygiene, sanitation, and medical care all require water. If a normal water source becomes contaminated, people may need to find another source and use an appropriate treatment method rather than simply continuing to use unsafe water.

Food storage also requires planning. Panic buying can temporarily remove supplies from stores, while taking far more than necessary can make shortages worse for everyone else. A sustainable survival strategy considers how long supplies will last, how they can be replaced, and how many people depend on them.

Medicine creates another difficult problem. A limited supply may have to be shared between multiple patients, while hospitals themselves may become overwhelmed. Real medical decisions depend on trained professionals, available treatments, patient condition, and public-health guidance. The quiz uses simplified fictional situations to explore the difficult resource choices that can arise during a prolonged emergency.

Evacuation, Shelter and Community Survival

Leaving an infected city may seem like the obvious solution, but evacuation can introduce its own risks. Roads can become crowded, fuel can become scarce, transportation networks can fail, and large groups of people can create additional exposure opportunities.

A remote location may provide fewer contacts and greater access to natural resources, but isolation also has major disadvantages. A small settlement may have limited medical care, electricity, communication, tools, skilled workers, and food production. A hospital or urban area may offer better medical resources while simultaneously creating greater exposure risk.

Shelter therefore involves more than finding a building. Long-term survival depends on clean water, sanitation, food production or reliable supplies, waste management, communication, security, medical knowledge, and people capable of maintaining essential systems.

Communities can also be more resilient than isolated individuals. A doctor, engineer, farmer, mechanic, teacher, electrician, or other skilled person can provide knowledge that becomes extremely valuable when normal services fail. Cooperation can increase survival capacity, but it also requires rules for distributing limited resources and managing health risks.

The Human Side of a Plague Survival Scenario

Disease outbreaks create difficult ethical questions because survival decisions rarely affect only one person. A family member may need treatment while supplies are limited. A stranger may need shelter after travelling from an infected area. A settlement may have to decide whether newcomers should be admitted, screened, quarantined, or refused.

These decisions create a conflict between individual compassion and collective risk. Helping someone can save a life, but uncontrolled contact can expose an entire household or community. At the same time, treating every unknown person as a threat can destroy cooperation and trust.

Fair rules become particularly important when resources are scarce. Decisions about medicine, food, shelter, or evacuation should ideally be based on consistent principles rather than simply giving advantages to friends, powerful people, or whoever happens to arrive first.

The Morality Score in this test does not determine whether you are a good or bad person. It simply reflects patterns in the choices you make. A highly compassionate decision can sometimes increase personal risk, while a harsh-looking decision may protect a larger group. The interesting part is seeing where you draw that line.

Plague Survival Test FAQ

How many decisions are in the test?

The Plague Survival Test contains 40 decisions. Every situation gives you four possible choices, and your selected answers determine your final Survival and Morality results.

How is the Survival Score calculated?

Every decision contributes to your Survival Score. The choices are scored according to how well they handle infection risk, resources, shelter, travel, medical situations, and long-term survival. Your final score is converted into a percentage.

What does the Morality Score measure?

Some decisions contain a separate morality value. These choices examine issues such as compassion, fairness, responsibility, loyalty, sacrifice, and helping other people. Your result ranges from Ruthless Pragmatist to Humanitarian.

What are the survival result levels?

Your Survival Score places you into one of five categories: Extremely Vulnerable, Short-Term Survivor, Survivor, Outbreak Survivor, or Master Survivor. The test also gives an estimated survival period based on your result.

Is this test medically accurate?

No. This is a fictional entertainment experience, not medical advice or a professional emergency-planning tool. Real infectious diseases differ greatly in transmission, symptoms, treatment, mortality, and prevention. During an actual outbreak, follow guidance from qualified medical and public-health authorities.

Can I take the test again?

Yes. Select TRY AGAIN after receiving your result and make different decisions. Comparing different attempts can show how changing your priorities affects both survival and morality.

Can I challenge my friends?

Yes. After completing the test, use the Challenge Your Friends button to share your result and see how other people handle the same outbreak decisions.
